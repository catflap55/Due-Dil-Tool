You will see a malware warning. That is normal.

“Start.command” Not Opened
Apple could not verify “Start.command” is free of malware that may harm your Mac or compromise your privacy.

Apple shows this for any start file downloaded from the internet that Apple did not sign. It is not saying this project is a virus. Do not delete the file. Do not double-click Start.command.

  1. On that warning, click Done.
  2. Click the Apple logo at the top-left of the screen.
  3. Click System Settings.
  4. Click Privacy & Security in the left list.
  5. Scroll down to the heading Security.
  6. You should see that Start.command was blocked. Click Open Anyway.
  7. Click Open Anyway again if another box appears. Enter the Mac password if asked.
  8. Go back to this Mac folder. Right-click (or Control-click) Start.command → Open.

A text window should appear. Leave it open. Do not type in it. Wait until it says Ready. Then the app opens. http://localhost:8765 and http://127.0.0.1:8765 are the same app. If one cannot connect, try the other. On some Macs only localhost works.

If Open Anyway is missing, you must trigger the warning once (you already did), then look again in Privacy & Security.
